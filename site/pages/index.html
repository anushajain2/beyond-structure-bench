A good crystal structure is not enough

How reliably do today's open ML force fields predict the structure, stiffness and thermal expansion of materials used in chips, and when they are wrong, what causes it?

MACE-MPA-0 places tantalum's lattice within 0.4 % of experiment and its stiffness within 6 %, yet predicts that it expands 2.8 times as much as it does when heated. The software and the numerics turned out not to be the cause. The model's vibrations change far too strongly with volume: its Grüneisen parameter is 4.1, against 1.6 inferred from experiment.

Three findings

From three models on 33 materials, compared with sourced experimental values and with DFT, and a separate study of six cases.

0.7–0.8 %

Structure is accurate; the data can't rank the models

Average lattice error for all three models on 30 non-layered materials; bulk-modulus error is 11–13 %. Against experiment, the benchmark resolves no difference between the models for either property.

29–35 %

Thermal expansion is where models fail, each in its own places

Average error at 300 K for MACE-MPA-0 and MatterSim. MACE overshoots refractory metals (tantalum +179 %); MatterSim overshoots semiconductors (InP +119 %, silicon +85 %).

γ = 4.1 vs 1.6

The large errors trace to how vibrations change with volume

MACE's Grüneisen parameter for tantalum, against experiment. In six selected cases, backend and numerical checks rule out the workflow, and the errors sit in the models' Grüneisen parameters, sometimes with a too-soft bulk modulus.
